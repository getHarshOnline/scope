# Evidence export and Reckon

Scope's research-export design lets a researcher examine evidence from real work while keeping private material under its owners' permissions. Scope signs an export containing only the derived evidence and exact source references allowed for that purpose, with exact references to every required permission. This is the admission check for the export. Real operation can reveal conditions a synthetic test misses; using that evidence for training needs its own permission.

An export can inform a separately frozen case, which Scope may propose to Reckon's official corpus. Reckon alone accepts the proposal and creates the canonical corpus record. The export, case, proposal and corpus record keep separate identities. Each further use, including evaluation, training, a ruling, release or activation, needs its own authority. The source owner's privacy, consent and permitted-use records govern each use; [admission](admission-and-trust.md) checks authority before access.

## An export is a membrane, not a query

The export family is a signed request from an admitted research requester and a signed export from Scope. It is a derived-evidence boundary: not a database query, a connector, a training pipeline or an execution episode. The request binds independently issued authority evidence, and the export contains only minimised derived evidence or exact opaque references that the request admits, as the same or a narrower projection of the request and its authority set. Check owner, caller, purpose, fixed source revision and member eligibility separately. Select permitted members before counts, limits, pagination, summaries or archive assembly; metadata and errors obey the same visibility. Refuse unknown revisions and unsupported required members, and disclose optional omissions only within the permitted scope. Bind order, tie-breaks, limits and any cursor to that scope and revision. Recheck permission and source invalidation before staged delivery. The field sketches are on [Record reference](record-reference.md).

Test overlapping identifiers from different sources, a member outside the grant, wrong caller or purpose, stale revision or cursor, empty selection and an unsupported option. Changes outside the selected scope must not leak through content, counts, errors or attachments. Repeat against the same fixed source to check deterministic order and limits, then revoke permission between staging and inspection. Keep the source and output identities, effective scope, allowed omissions, retention and invalidation rules with the export.

**Permitted use is an allowlist, not a ladder.** Recorded source or runtime use, context retention, evaluation, calibration, training, fine-tuning, a derivative checkpoint or model, reusable generalisation and publication are separate permissions. Runtime use does not imply retention or evaluation; evaluation does not imply training; training does not imply a derivative, generalisation, publication, release or activation. Every authority role the export policy requires (source owner, data controller, relationship, confidentiality, consent, rights, and research, training, derivative, generalisation and publication use) must resolve to an independently valid record. Scope cannot manufacture any of them by re-signing, and empty selectors mean empty, never all.

**One provenance class per export.** Real operational evidence and synthetic simulation evidence, such as a RawFish run, are compared only as separately identified corpora and are never pooled under an unlabelled split. Raw prompts, private source payloads, hidden reasoning, credentials, secrets and unrestricted owner records are never export fields. A bounded derivation names its exact inputs, transform, output hash, loss and redaction, and cannot present an inferred outcome as owner truth. Omissions and unknowns are listed explicitly; an unknown is never coerced into absent, false or zero. Each export carries its transform profile, dataset split, leakage report and selection-bias report, and its retention, deletion and expiry.

**Execution evidence travels as opaque references.** An export may reference a direct Claude Code or Codex path, or a Gas Town path with no organising owner, when each item is an exact admitted Pale or owner record; it does not need, and must never fabricate, an organisation identifier or an episode. An episode that does exist is referenced only by its full identity and exact revision. Execution-path, delegation and terminal-leaf evidence travel as opaque reference and hash pairs to Pale-owned records; Scope binds and compares them but never enumerates their values or parses product names into path structure, and an empty delegation list means the selected path had no delegation. Outcome evidence enters only by exact owner reference; a missing outcome stays an explicit unknown.

**Invalidation follows the source.** Deletion, revocation, supersession, authority expiry or a change in a source frontier invalidates dependent exports according to the bound policy. Scope records the invalidation or tombstone and requires the destination owner's custody and deletion evidence where a copy was materialised; it cannot claim to recall bytes it does not control. An export grants no online read, model update, candidate publication, Pale admission or activation. Private evidence cannot become reusable package or Skill content or a derivative model without its own generalisation or derivative grant, and a reward- or candidate-generating evaluator can never be the only evaluator for a promotion.

A Causality research consumer requires the affected owners' approval, a defined experiment and candidate class, and separate data permission. Under that proposed connection, Causality owns only a preregistered matched comparison, MUON and HENA keep their own research artifacts, and Reckon provides evaluation and, for a learned promotion, the required curated case and recorded decision. A result returns to Pale only as evidence for a new admission decision, never as a Scope memory proposal. Describing this connection grants none of its required permissions.

## A case is proposed to Reckon's corpus; Reckon alone mints

```text
owner projection or reference
  -> Scope admits a case and freezes a secret-free revision
  -> Scope verifies source, controller, purpose, consent, privacy, rights and model-use records
  -> bounded case proposal to Reckon
  -> Reckon's curator disposition
  -> for an eligible accepted case only: Reckon mints the canonical record and version
  -> exact version plus an authorised-use grant
  -> Reckon's corpus-use receipt, and Scope's own consumption record
```

Scope may assemble and freeze one bounded, secret-free case revision from its admitted projections and exact owner references. Before proposing it, Scope verifies the independent source-owner, controller, purpose, confidentiality, consent, privacy, rights and model-use records and keeps their exact references, hashes, scope, destination, validity and supersession state; it neither issues nor widens them. Missing, stale, expired, conflicting or purpose-mismatched authority yields a fail-closed non-proposal. The proposal carries only the frozen case's identity and digest, provenance, omissions and unknowns, permission references, the requested corpus purpose and the requested retention and use scope; credentials, raw private payloads, hidden reasoning, mutable source pointers and unbounded provider access are prohibited.

Reckon owns curator disposition, minting and the corpus-use receipt. Scope records the exact disposition by reference. Before consumption, verify the case and input revision, digest, accepted reference answer, current status and permission for the consumer, purpose, scope, destination, model use and time. Keep original annotations, corrections, adjudications and accepted answers distinct; a correction needs a successor revision. Fix dataset membership and partitions by exact case identities, revisions and digests, grouping related sources or derivatives to prevent leakage. Unreviewed, deferred, conflicting, denied or withdrawn items remain outside the accepted set. An unavailable item leaves a coverage gap rather than silently acquiring a replacement.

Scope records its own consumption and matches the fixed input and run identity to Reckon's use receipt. Inspecting an old receipt, reconstructing its inputs and using those bytes in a new run require separate permission. An unchanged digest, earlier receipt, restored snapshot or cached grant cannot override current withdrawal or expiry. Hold dependent use when current authority cannot be established. Before exposing a result, recheck the exact recipient, result and current grant, resolving any withdrawal race under the owner's ordering rules. Preserve earlier receipts only as retention permits, record withheld delivery separately from processing already observed, and invalidate dependent evidence from exact lifecycle references.

Test matching use, permission for a different purpose or destination, an expired or withdrawn revision, a receipt for another run, a restored but now-ineligible copy, a lost delivery acknowledgement followed by withdrawal, and permission changing between acceptance and delivery. Refusal exposes no protected result. A permitted replacement needs separately accepted inputs and its own receipt. An ordinary research comparison may use a separately permitted Scope export where its experiment allows it. A learned promotion still requires its curated case in Reckon's shared set.

## Use published results without a live Reckon dependency

Pale and Scope use published, versioned Reckon results when making decisions. Scope may select a published calibration table or case set for a permitted use, records its exact version, and continues from that version when Reckon is unavailable. Scope owns how the result informs its decision; source permissions, eligibility and the learned-promotion rule still apply. The consumer must pin the published result format, check it at use and exercise an outage with that retained version in the [go-live rehearsal](improvement-and-go-live.md#go-live-is-a-full-rehearsal-through-rollback-done-offline).

## Reckon evaluates learned changes and preserves the decision

Every learned change requires Reckon's evaluation, a curated case and the responsible owner's preserved decision. Ordinary work and changes the owner authors directly remain independent. Reckon is not Scope's runtime, store, authority or online learner. No Reckon process writes Scope's source, ledger, episode, score, proposal, deletion, supersession or release state; Scope never imports Reckon's Python runtime into its hot path or treats Reckon's in-memory store as durable memory; and Reckon never gets live Scope reads, provider access, a caller identity, credentials or a mutation path.

| Concern | Owner | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Case assembly, admission and freezing | Scope | Creates no corpus membership |
| Source, controller, purpose, rights and model-use admission | Each named authority | Scope and Reckon verify and never issue, widen or infer |
| Curator disposition and minting | Reckon | Mint certifies corpus identity and curation, not truth |
| Corpus authorisation and use | The permission owner; Reckon for its use receipt; Scope for its consumption record | Use never selects evaluation or promotion |
| Evaluator candidates and artifacts | Reckon | Each candidate has a distinct identity, version, source digest, numeric domain, question and budget; each artifact is signed, content-addressed and carries uncertainty and failed or unknown cases |
| Scope's ranking | Scope | Reckon cannot replace or wrap the production scorer |
| Scope's experiments, comparisons and proposals | Scope | Scope preregisters and proposes only its own bounded change |
| The ruling on an adaptive promotion | The responsible owner decides; Reckon preserves that decision | Never automatic; the owner decides first |
| Scope's release | Scope | Needs the recorded decision and its own release checks |

Every evaluator candidate binds a unique identifier that is never reused, its source revision and content digest, formula and configuration version, input schema, numeric domain and missing-value rules, deterministic reference vectors and parity tolerance, the outcome evidence it may use, dataset split and leakage controls, resource limits and failure disposition, and its output schema and digest. An evaluator name or the phrase "causal score" is not evidence of causal identification; a candidate may carry a causal label only under Reckon's accepted causal-evidence profile, and otherwise it is called ablation, attribution, proxy or ranking evidence. Signed, zero, near-zero, non-finite and empty inputs need explicit behaviour, and an undefined domain, unsafe normalisation or missing adversarial vectors makes a candidate ineligible rather than coercing a score. [Improvement and go-live](improvement-and-go-live.md) separates Scope's scorer, retrieval-policy and memory candidates from Pale's instruction candidates; Reckon owns the published evaluator contract each comparison pins.

Scope preregisters the experiment and freezes its permitted evidence. For a learned candidate it binds the curated case version, and Reckon evaluates the candidate and signs the result. Bind the source and configuration, data lineage, method and prompt, workflow and adapter, model settings, validator, output schema, execution mode and reference-vector parity. Keep the case, assessment and provider attempt distinct, with raw output, normalised result, evaluator observation and accepted decision separately inspectable. Stage replay fixes upstream outputs; end-to-end replay regenerates them under the candidate. Exclude later labels from decision-time inputs, prevent live effects during replay, and count failed attempts, retries, fallback, shadow calls and review in cost and coverage. A required evaluation that fails, is partial or stale, or cannot be verified holds the dependent proposal. The responsible owner then decides; Reckon preserves the exact decision and returns its reference. Scope verifies it before its separate release. The [adaptive go-live rehearsal](improvement-and-go-live.md#go-live-is-a-full-rehearsal-through-rollback-done-offline) exercises this whole path. A Reckon outage holds new learned promotion while ordinary Scope work continues.

Scope may also offer Reckon a bounded projection of its edit lineage, when both owners select that relationship: Scope then produces and signs each projection instance against Reckon's published schema, and keeps every original record and all mutation, decision and release authority. Ordinary owner-authored releases remain outside the learned-promotion path. Reckon publishes the record-store protocol: versioned write and retrieval, observation, probes and bounded run export. Scope pins that published contract rather than deriving it from these operation names.

For a comparison across evaluator versions, retain each historical observation with its evaluator, rubric, metric, schema, cases and available evidence. Admit a separate comparison for a shared question, matching case identities and revisions and fixing candidate outputs, independent references and scoring policies. Check common units, criterion meanings, thresholds and missing-case rules; equal labels or numeric ranges do not establish the same measure. Hold the evaluator fixed to compare candidates and outputs fixed to compare evaluators. If both change and interaction matters, score both candidates with both evaluators. Compute a joint difference only on a justified common measure, retaining each evaluator's original findings.

Test changed rubric meaning under the same score label, changed case coverage and an unavailable historical output. Preserve original denominators and disclose incomplete pairs; a matched-subset result applies only to that subset. Scope retains the original artifact references and keeps a proposal that depends on an unresolved comparison pending. A separately admitted comparison that can answer its own question may proceed.

## Role confusion is rejected at every step

Tests must show that a Reckon process trying to read or change Scope's store is denied; that an artifact whose candidate, configuration or data changed after preregistration is rejected before comparison; that a candidate failing parity or its numeric domain is recorded ineligible with no fallback score; that a scalar outcome without its underlying terminal and acceptance evidence is excluded; that a passing experiment produces at most a proposal while production stays byte-identical until a separate release; that a rejection, timeout or partial evaluation leaves the scorer and ledger unchanged; that a research run presented as a Scope experiment, or the reverse, is rejected; that evidence from a path with no organising owner assigned a synthetic episode, mixed real and synthetic provenance, or evaluation permission widened to training is rejected; that Reckon attempting training, candidate selection, live judgment, foreign persistence, admission, approval or release is denied; that a case with missing, stale or conflicting permission, credentials, hidden reasoning, a mutable source identity or a widened purpose is rejected before review; and that a proposal, disposition, mint, use grant, use receipt, evaluation, ruling or release is never presented as another of those states.
