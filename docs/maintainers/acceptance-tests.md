# Acceptance tests

No Scope interface becomes callable until its owner and lifecycle are ratified, its schemas validate against their hashes, and its acceptance tests pass. The [first independent checkpoint](contracts.md) includes refusal of execution fields. The execution-bound tests below apply after the successor contract is published. Standalone strict mode has its own proof without Pale or Counsel. The [go-live certificate](improvement-and-go-live.md#go-live-is-a-full-rehearsal-through-rollback-done-offline) also requires integrated full mode, the [retrieval falsifiers](retrieval.md) and the adaptive rehearsal on the exact build. The [Dolt cases below](#dolt-import-isolation-and-recovery-cases) distinguish the recorded boundary probes from the required Scope tests. The full suite has yet to run on a selected release.

## The core negative paths

1. A caller claiming another role, controller scope or relationship scope without a valid grant is denied before any source byte is read.
2. A stale or revoked required source makes context-critical work fail closed. Where policy permits a limited retrieval result, the exact signed bytes and visible limitations travel together. A revoked or unauthorised item remains excluded.
3. Two writers racing on the same revision leave one winner and one conflict; there is never a last-write-wins update.
4. A crash before or after a semantic change, an inbox acknowledgement, a use-receipt commit or an outbox publication replays to exactly one durable result.
5. A redelivered event, import or use receipt creates no duplicate state, thanks to compound identity and idempotency.
6. A superseded or deleted source invalidates dependent bundles and views, leaves a tombstone preserving lineage, and leaves unrelated canon intact.
7. Compaction that drops a material caveat or source digest is rejected, and deleting the derived view leaves the source evidence rebuildable.
8. A model proposing a Skill or configuration change leaves the canonical source unchanged until its owner accepts and releases it.
9. Diagnostic queries scoped by role or controller cannot infer hidden nodes through counts, maxima, timing, errors, telemetry, receipts or replay cursors.
10. With Pale removed, Scope still resolves strict-mode requests under [its own signed grants](admission-and-trust.md). A Pale binding alone cannot admit a standalone request. Restore a snapshot and cache with valid artifacts from an earlier integrated run. Supply a declared memory-host profile and a valid Scope retrieval grant. With the configured [standalone memory-host route](architecture.md#scope-builds-and-signs-every-answer) present but no separate host-invocation and disclosure permission, a new full-only request is refused. A request permitting strict mode can receive a model-free bundle. Verify that neither request invokes the host or uses the retained model artifacts. As the positive control, supply the exact permitted host, profile, input and separate grants and verify full mode without a Pale daemon; revoke either grant before delivery and verify refusal.
11. A research export with missing or expired authority, widened use, mixed real and synthetic provenance, a raw prompt, private payload or hidden reasoning, an invented outcome, leakage across splits or an undeclared omission is denied.
12. Evidence from a direct Claude Code or Codex path, or from Gas Town with no organising owner, enters only by exact owner references; any attempt to synthesise an organisation or an episode is denied, and export invalidation records uncertainty and downstream custody instead of claiming remote bytes were recalled.
13. A semantic-inferred edge with missing evidence, profile or confidence, a forged occurrence or frontier reference, or a claim that an acknowledgement proves an effect, response or outcome is rejected, and nothing changes in the owner's or Pale's genealogy.
14. Reject a source helper that leaks credentials or returns an unbounded or mutable projection. A helper result or wake cannot stand in for a committed owner publication. Scope’s outbox certifies only its own committed revision.
15. A case lacking exact source, controller, purpose, rights or model-use evidence, containing secrets or presented as already minted is not proposed to Reckon; a rejected, deferred or tombstoned case, a stale version or missing use authorisation cannot enter a bundle or experiment; minting, use, evaluation, ruling, release and activation are tested as independent transitions.
16. Equal sealed inputs producing a different snapshot digest, a partially visible build, or one request seeing mixed semantic, policy or snapshot identities quarantines the candidate and keeps or forward-reactivates the previous snapshot.
17. Affinity changing an admitted edge, provenance, tombstone, source fact or genealogy, or a planner considering an ineligible item before the controller, relationship, purpose and model-use wall, fails conformance.
18. Strict mode needing an embedding, model summary, learned link or live provider, a memory-host loss silently weakening policy, or an unavailable route reported as a success fails the mode and degradation proof.
19. A vector candidate relying only on filtering afterwards, lacking an exact-scan comparison, or leaking excluded populations through results, traversal, timing, cardinality, cache or error shape beyond the profile is disabled and certification is rejected.
20. A decision record that lacks the complete eligible, action and policy evidence while claiming replay, or a lawful deletion that leaves payload in an embedding, affinity or cache or calls a hash reconstructible content, is rejected.
21. An evaluation, Reckon custody, owner release, genealogy occurrence or Pale activation that activates Scope's retrieval policy by itself, or a Scope activation that changes Pale's state, is rejected as a collapsed lifecycle.
22. A repository alias, remote URL or checkout path presented as identity, a Git location bound to the wrong repository projection, or a checkout or worktree path stored outside `resource_locations` is rejected before the V005 generation can replace the active one.

## The cross-cutting proofs

- Scope publishes a role-typed resource and participant manifest and its exact private workspace instance and binding, without copying source truth or Scope state.
- Structural evidence flows from source to [CodeGraph](architecture.md#code-structure-flows-one-way-from-source-through-pale) to Pale to an optional Scope reference, and Scope's reverse questions go only through Pale; there are no direct CodeGraph writes.
- Continuity, freshness, merge and conflict, checkpoints and replay, migration and deletion hold per resource without Scope becoming a universal session or application-state store.
- Removing an interface or plugin, and bootstrapping a clean adopter, preserves Scope's records and every source owner's authority.
- With Scope unavailable, admit the owner’s committed publication through Pale using the selected same-transaction outbox or qualified native log. Exercise both publication sources and reject a wake or a log that cannot prove the commit and replay boundary. On the changing-source path, read back Scope’s separate derived revision and outbox. [Direct and changing-source routes](architecture.md#three-paths-touch-scope-and-none-stands-in-for-another)
- With Pale absent, import a permitted fixture snapshot signed by an enrolled owner. Repeat with a named adapter and an exact active delegation. Substitute a strict retrieval grant for the delegation, then try a delegation for a different namespace and a revoked delegation. Verify refusal before parsing the body and read back the prior generation. [Import admission](source-imports.md#admission-comes-before-parsing)
- Case admission, corpus-use permission, Reckon's disposition, minting and use receipts, evaluation and preservation of the decision stay separate, including every safe non-mint ending and every stale, withdrawn or tombstoned use rejection.
- Run an ordinary research comparison without Reckon and verify that its declared gates govern it. Present that result as sufficient to promote a learned candidate and verify refusal until Reckon supplies the evaluation, curated case and recorded decision. Then compare Reckon’s signed artifacts across evaluator versions. Change the rubric under the same score label, shift coverage, and withhold a replay input in separate runs. Verify that Scope keeps both original references, leaves the dependent proposal pending and leaves the active policy unchanged. Admit a matched comparison for a narrower question and verify that it leaves the broader proposal pending. [Evaluation boundary](evidence-export-and-reckon.md)
- At proposal validation, supply a valid comparison for a different artifact revision under the same evaluator and candidate labels. Repeat with the exact comparison after Scope's use grant is revoked. Each leaves the dependent proposal pending and the active policy unchanged. In the control, a matching, currently admitted comparison can satisfy the evaluation check. The ruling and release still need their own authority. [Cross-version evidence](evidence-export-and-reckon.md#reckon-evaluates-learned-changes-and-preserves-the-decision)
- Revoke a required admission after Reckon records an accepted disposition but before minting. Read back the disposition and verify that no version minted. In a separate run, withdraw a minted version after its first authorised use while an evaluation is in flight. Replaying the original use receipt must neither admit a new read nor make the late artifact eligible for comparison or proposal. Read back Reckon's withdrawal and Scope's invalidation separately. [Corpus handoff](evidence-export-and-reckon.md#scope-corpus-handoff)
- Organisational context keeps source-qualified groups, trees, leadership, roles, services and product relations without filling gaps, merging look-alikes, widening disclosure or turning relevance into authority, and stale or conflicting relations stay explicit.
- Snapshots compile reproducibly with exact semantic, policy, permission and toolchain pins, activate atomically, pin one per request, drain old readers and roll back forward; partial or mixed state never becomes visible.
- Pause a resolve after pinning its snapshot and before bundle issuance. Let a source tombstone or grant revocation take effect, in separate runs. Verify that Scope withholds the affected result. Use a routine snapshot swap with the authority intact as the control. Then issue and read a valid bundle before invalidation. Replay its exact read after invalidation, including from a restored cache. Verify refusal without disclosure or substitution, and keep the earlier lawful receipts only within retention policy. Run the exact-read case under both admission modes. [Bundle read](episodes-and-use.md#a-bundle-is-read-back-as-the-exact-bytes-that-were-bound)
- Prove strict retrieval with all model-made artifacts removed. In the integrated full fixture, select a memory-host profile and admit the Scope caller, then withhold or revoke Pale's invocation or source-disclosure authority. Repeat with admission for Counsel's Judgment Host alone. Verify that these cases send no source content and start no memory-host call. At the host boundary, substitute the selected profile or source projection under otherwise valid invocation evidence. Verify refusal before model processing and no candidate for Scope. [Memory-host admission](architecture.md#scope-builds-and-signs-every-answer)
- Admit the integrated invocation and return a valid candidate, then withhold Scope's artifact admission. The candidate cannot enter a snapshot. Repeat with a wrong controller, a stale source and an expired grant. In separate runs, revoke input disclosure and invocation authority after dispatch but before admission. Each late candidate must stay out of the snapshot. In a fresh valid run, capture an admitted artifact in a cache or snapshot, then revoke the grant governing use and verify that the captured artifact cannot be reused. Where strict retrieval can still answer, compare a request allowing strict fallback with one requiring full mode. The first returns a separately receipted strict result. The second fails closed. [Retrieval](retrieval.md)
- At artifact admission, substitute a Judgment Host result with the same model and provenance envelope. Repeat with a Memory and Embedding Host result from a different deployment or invocation while keeping the source and profile labels the same. Then alter the bytes of a valid result and keep its original evidence. Reject each before snapshot, index or ranking use. Restore a cache containing the same substitutions and check the same refusal. Use an exact result from the admitted memory-host invocation as the positive control. [Artifact provenance](retrieval.md#strict-mode-needs-no-model-full-mode-may-use-counsel)
- With model artifacts already in a snapshot and cache, interrupt a host operation required by the selected full path. Read back a separately receipted strict result where policy permits it, with model artifacts absent from traversal and ranking. A full-required request refuses. In a separate run, keep the host healthy, retire the producing profile and restore the old snapshot and cache. Verify that a new request cannot use their model artifacts, even if the original source and grants are still valid. Read back the unchanged source and the audit references still allowed by retention. [Mode and artifact rules](retrieval.md#strict-mode-needs-no-model-full-mode-may-use-counsel)
- Eligibility and required context come before every route and fusion step, and vector output, traversal influence and observable isolation meet the declared level against an exact-scan oracle.
- Governed-window evidence is complete, lawful deletion removes payload, embeddings, active learned links and caches, and decisions become explicitly unreconstructible when their inputs are gone.
- An admitted use report still yields only an attributed [use receipt](episodes-and-use.md) when no model trace exists. Receipt, diagnostics and scoring projections keep it separate from model-visible input, causal impact and owner outcomes.
- Run independent resolve, exact read and use receipt under standalone admission and the separately published integrated admission. Supply a complete predecessor key, a partial execution binding and execution-reservation fields on their own in separate negative vectors. Each schema rejects execution fields even when the rest of the record validates. Read back the initial diagnostic snapshot and deltas, including after an unbound owner outcome. Admitted context activity is visible. Episode identity, episode counters and owner-outcome events are absent, and the event adds no outcome weight. [Data model](data-model.md) · [Diagnostics](diagnostics.md)
- The [adaptive rehearsal](improvement-and-go-live.md#go-live-is-a-full-rehearsal-through-rollback-done-offline) compares the selected candidates under the same hard budgets, splits and fixtures. The selected adaptive case runs through ruling, custody, release, simulated activation and rollback and leaves the deterministic policy active.
- Scope and Pale release and activate independently through exact compatibility and readiness evidence; genealogy receives only generic lifecycle occurrences with opaque references, and an owner's direct event publication stays independent.

## Execution-bound proofs

Run these after the [episode issuer and successor contract](episodes-and-use.md#which-owner-gives-an-episode-its-identity-is-still-open) are settled and published. While the independent checkpoint is selected, its refusal cases above still apply.

- Bind the issuing owner and namespace, execution identity, reservation and generation fence before opening an episode; a short run ID or parent reference alone cannot identify it. Test equal run IDs from different issuers, exact open and bind replay after a later revision, and concurrent opens for one identity under different request identifiers. A lookup must create no provisional record. Bind one handoff against the exact predecessor revision and digest; test a matching revision with the wrong digest, then read back the unchanged record. Inspect dependent effect counts separately from receipts. Read back one initial revision and the original receipts. Reuse the open request identifier with a changed body and verify refusal. [Episode opening and binding](episodes-and-use.md#an-episode-is-opened-before-resolve-and-bound-once-to-its-handoff)
- Deliver a terminal event from the wrong owner, with a stale fence, and against a provisional open with no bound handoff in separate runs. Read back unchanged episode and score inputs. Then deliver the committed owner event against the bound handoff and replay it, including under a second admitted delivery identity for the same owner event version. Read back one closure, one evidence application and the durable disposition for each delivery. [Episode closure](episodes-and-use.md#an-episode-ends-once-either-closed-or-expired) · [Owner event inbox](durability-and-recovery.md#owner-events-enter-through-a-durable-inbox)
- Read the executor's committed result through its authorised route and match its verified reference and digest to the original publication, exact attempt and handoff. Test a transport observation with the owner record withheld, changed bytes under the expected reference, another publication's genuine result and a predecessor result substituted for a replacement. Missing owner evidence leaves the dependent transition open. Keep Scope closure and any parent-owner acceptance separate; advance the parent revision between validation and commit and verify that no stale parent decision occurs. Test both arrival orders and read each owner after the first commit and after both. An episode closure cannot accept parent work. Race an eligible expiry request and a valid close against the same open revision. Force each to win in a separate run and read back exactly one terminal transition. An exact expiry replay returns its prior disposition. Reuse of its request identity with changed bytes is refused. When expiry wins, a different-handoff event leaves the episode unchanged. Repeated delivery of the exact owner event yields one late evidence entry, with the original expiry, no closing time and unchanged score inputs. [Episode lifecycle](episodes-and-use.md)
- For the deferred assisted path, check the selected handoff’s source owners, roles, exact revisions and delivered bytes, recipient, purpose, freshness and current permission both at dispatch and use. Test a missing required input, wrong owner, insufficient budget and a delayed handoff after expiry or revocation. A refresh with changed bytes needs a separately admitted successor. Test missing acceptance records and substitutions from another handoff or expired generation; each refusal must leave the child effect count unchanged. Verify that authorised degradation dispatches with signed evidence and both Scope receipts. It has no bundle read or use receipt. Remove Scope before open and before bind in separate runs. Both assisted variants must stop in each case. A separately admitted independent handoff makes no Scope call. [Episode sequence](episodes-and-use.md)
- Crash before and after an episode close. Verify replay produces one durable closure and one evidence application. A replay with the same delivery key and changed digest, reference or schema is quarantined.

## Repository-location migration fixtures

These fixtures run against the V005 candidate migration before its generation may become active. Each begins with an owner-issued repository snapshot, one immutable codebase `resources.resource_id`, its one-to-one `git_repos` row and an authority-backed canonical-location head. Each fixture asserts the database constraints as well as the importer result; a rejected candidate leaves the previous generation, resource identity and canonical head byte-for-byte unchanged. An authorised migration from experimental location tables also tests the [old-row disposition and removal receipt](data-model.md) before retiring those tables.

| Fixture | Hostile change | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repository rename | A fresh owner snapshot changes the repository's human name or slug, or an importer tries to create a second `git_repos` row for the new slug | A genuine rename updates only the resource's human alias and admitted source revision; the same `resource_id`, repository subtype row, locations and canonical head survive. The second repository row fails the one-to-one key. |
| Moved checkout | A checkout moves from one local path to another, or an importer tries to write the new path on `git_repos` | The old location is retired and replaced by a new `git_checkout` location admitted under the same repository resource. There is no repository path column. The existing head remains until a separately authorised successor selects the new location. |
| Multiple clones | Two owner-reported clones have different paths and remotes for the same repository, or a path is offered as a new repository identity | Both clones may exist as distinct `git_checkout` locations bound to one `git_repo_resource_id`; neither becomes canonical by recency or reachability. A second repository identity derived from either path is rejected. |
| Worktree | An active worktree is admitted, then the importer supplies a path directly on `git_worktrees`, reuses its location for another worktree, points it at a checkout location, or pairs it with another repository | The path exists only on one `git_worktree` location. `git_worktrees.location_id` is unique and its composite foreign key accepts only that same repository and `git_worktree` type; every mismatched variant is rejected. |
| Mismatched repository or location | Repository A is paired with repository B's location, or resource A's canonical head names resource B's location | The typed repository/location foreign key and the same-resource canonical-head foreign key reject the candidate with no partial state. |
| Stale owner snapshot | An older source revision attempts a rename, path move, clone or worktree addition, location retirement or canonical-head change after a newer generation is active | Admission rejects the stale snapshot before mutation. The current alias, repository projection, location set, generation and owner-authorised head remain unchanged. |

The successful cases among these fixtures also prove that non-identity changes do not churn `resource_id`: a rename keeps it; a move replaces only a location; multiple clones share it; and a worktree qualifies one typed location. No fixture may reintroduce `repo_path`, `plan_dir`, `data_dir`, `website_repo`, `github_org`, `github_repo`, `planning_path` or any compatibility alias.

## Dolt import isolation and recovery cases

These cases make the [import commit boundary](source-imports.md#activation-is-short-ordered-and-anchored) concrete. The 9 November 2026 probes used synthetic data with Dolt 1.88.1 at source revision `14b38e279fc65cac8b40dc9f7814c10db4604916`. Embedded CLI calls ran sequentially against a shared working set and stage. A separate SQL-server probe used two overlapping writer transactions and an independent observer, with `autocommit=1`, `dolt_transaction_commit=0` and Go's `go-sql-driver/mysql` 1.9.3. Neither route exercised Scope, its intended `mysql_async` client or its independent rollback anchor. Implement and run the cases against Scope's selected server and client before relying on its writer fence or recovery.

Start each case from a committed empty `imports(namespace_id, generation)` table, keyed by namespace, and an empty `unrelated(id)` table. Writer A commits its SQL insertion of `('alpha', 2)` before staging that candidate for a Dolt commit. Writer B adds `('beta', 9)` to the same physical table and `(11)` to `unrelated`, with its SQL commit placed as the table below specifies. Read both tables at the exact candidate hash using `AS OF`, then read the working set separately. A namespace boundary does not isolate either the shared table or the branch's staging area.

| Case | Interleaving and Dolt operation | Rows found at the candidate commit in the recorded probes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `before_stage` | B commits its SQL changes before A stages `imports`; A commits with `-m` | `imports`: `alpha:2`, `beta:9`; `unrelated`: empty |
| `after_stage` | A stages `imports` before B commits its SQL changes; A commits with `-m` without restaging | `imports`: `alpha:2`; `unrelated`: empty |
| `after_stage_restage` (`restage` in the server probe) | Repeat `after_stage`, then stage `imports` again before committing with `-m` | `imports`: `alpha:2`, `beta:9`; `unrelated`: empty |
| `after_stage_shared_stage` (`shared_stage` in the server probe) | Repeat `after_stage`, then stage `unrelated` without restaging `imports`; commit with `-m` | `imports`: `alpha:2`; `unrelated`: `11` |
| `after_stage_commit_all` (`auto_stage` in the server probe) | Repeat `after_stage`, then use the auto-staging commit `-am` | `imports`: `alpha:2`, `beta:9`; `unrelated`: `11` |

In all five cases the working set contains both namespaces and `unrelated:11`; a correct working-set read cannot establish what the candidate commit contains. Also keep the two controls from the first boundary probe: committing only A's SQL transaction leaves Dolt `HEAD` unchanged while `alpha:2` appears in the working set; committing the isolated `imports` table first leaves that pinned commit unchanged after a later SQL insertion of `beta:9`. Read pinned commits again after a later branch commit. The embedded staging probe checked this for `after_stage`; the server probe checked all five cases.

For Scope's acceptance test, bind the intended change set and expected parent before staging. The writer fence must protect the entire set through the Dolt commit, including other namespaces sharing a table and other staged tables. Verify the exact committed rows before exposing the new generation or its receipts. Extra rows, a changed parent or an unidentifiable commit keep the import hidden; they cannot be accepted merely because the SQL transaction succeeded.

For lost-reply recovery, discard the candidate commit's first server response bytes and return EOF to the client before it records the hash. In separate runs, make a later working-set write and advance the branch with another commit. Find the candidate in history by its expected parent and exact changes in both tables, then read it by hash. The recorded server probe found the original candidate after the head advanced and left that head unchanged during read-back. Scope must issue no second commit to discover the first, and an absent or ambiguous match must keep the import hidden. Finding the commit permits only the outstanding recovery step; the checkpoint and anchor conditions still govern when readers can see the result.

## The remaining suites

- **Schemas and operations.** Hash, unknown-major and bounded-size vectors for every published record. Publishing a valid state schema absent from the callable-operation inventory creates no command; reject an undeclared operation even when its record schema is known.
- **Identity.** Forged roles, shared-user callers and wrong principal, controller, relationship, purpose or classification are denied before source bytes are read; standalone grant transfer, replay, expiry and revocation fail; substituting a Pale binding or hash fails.
- **Predecessor store.** Offer a synthetic old memory folder whose database name and port match the planned Scope binding. Prove that bootstrap and migration refuse it before opening any provider configuration, connecting to the store or touching credential files. Verify that the folder and Scope state remain unchanged and diagnostics reveal no private path or credential detail. Repeat with a separately approved clean machine binding as the positive control. [Credential boundary](admission-and-trust.md#no-credential-ever-crosses-into-scope)
- **Criticality.** With an optional source unavailable after refresh, run confidential drafting under an owner-signed context-enhanced requirement that permits degradation. Verify that the result states its limits and carries a degradation receipt, with no irreversible disclosure, native mutation or change to authority, canon or lifecycle. Repeat without permission to degrade, with the source marked required and with an attempt to publish under the drafting authority. Verify that each dependent action stops. Use [the task owner's criticality rules](episodes-and-use.md#scope-context-freshness) for the other task classes.
- **Fencing and crashes.** Crash injection before and after every semantic change, import, inbox commit, acknowledgement and use-receipt commit.
- **Import visibility.** Interrupt each [anchor advance and Dolt commit](source-imports.md) and drop each anchor acknowledgement after the advance. On restart, read back the exact checkpoint pair and verify that the new generation and receipt outbox stay hidden until both advances settle. Plant a different hash at the same sequence and verify quarantine. After recovery, exact replay returns the original receipt pair.
- **Import commit scope.** Run the [Dolt import isolation and recovery cases](#dolt-import-isolation-and-recovery-cases), including both overlapping writers, shared staging and a lost commit reply. The exact checkpoint changes must survive or the import must stay hidden.
- **Import replay after advancement.** Commit an import, then advance the namespace with another import. Replay the first admission and verify byte-identical receipts. Submit the first body under a fresh valid admission and verify one anchored `duplicate` pair pointing to both original receipts. Replay that admission and verify it returns its own pair. Changed reuse of an admission identity, import identifier or semantic key fails closed. A different import with a stale predecessor conflicts. Repeat while the original receipt is still gated and with a caller outside the authorised scope. Verify that no early or unauthorised receipt is exposed and that no retry reapplies a snapshot or advances the generation.
- **Replay.** Imports, event references and use receipts replay idempotently, and the same delivery key with a changed canonical digest, reference or schema is quarantined.
- **Omission.** Run the [snapshot activation cases](source-imports.md#omission-never-deletes) under all three Scope admission branches. Omit an existing item from a partial snapshot and verify that it survives. Supply an explicit owner-authorised tombstone as the positive removal control. Attempt policy-derived removal without complete-set coverage, with the wrong owner or scope, and with incomplete absence, grace or reconciliation evidence; each must leave the previous generation unchanged. Include expired and out-of-range policies. After valid staging, supersede the selected complete-set policy head before the generation swap. Verify that the previous generation stays active and no policy-derived tombstone is applied. For an accepted import, read back the checked head reference, hash and epoch. Assert the required tombstone generation and evidence pairs and the chunk bound by the signed body.
- **Deletion and supersession.** Dependents invalidate, tombstones keep lineage, unrelated canon survives, derived views rebuild.
- **Compaction.** Information-loss and access-revocation tests; no source or accepted claim is ever pruned by a model.
- **Side channels.** Counts, maxima, timing, errors, telemetry, receipts and diagnostic replay across principal, controller, relationship and role.
- **Standalone.** The strict-mode proof above also runs without an organiser, Gas Town, a provider adapter, Reckon or any particular interface.
- **Replacement.** Remove the repository, broker and optional adapters one at a time and verify that unaffected paths remain available. Replacing a provider adapter preserves its contract. Removing the diagnostic stream preserves semantic results.
- **Concurrency.** Independent conflict domains commit concurrently without sharing authorisation or transaction state; a projection carrying a credential reference, configuration path, shell fragment or unbound route is rejected without partial change; a route reference swapped across owners or admissions is rejected; a stale, partial, hash-invalid or tombstone-incomplete generation leaves the previous generation active with one auditable disposition; drift detection produces evidence only; the repository-location migration fixtures above run under both sequential import and competing-generation schedules.
- **Diagnostics.** The suites listed on [Diagnostics](diagnostics.md).
- **Recovery.** The suites listed on [Durability and recovery](durability-and-recovery.md).
- **Reckon boundary.** The suites listed on [Evidence export and Reckon](evidence-export-and-reckon.md).
